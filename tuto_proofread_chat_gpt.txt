		The nation is in disarray. The economy is failing and political tensions are rising. The [red]*working class*[/red] faces a dismantled welfare system, the [blue]*capitalists*[/blue] are losing their profits, the middle class is gradually fading, and the state is sinking into a deep deficit. In these troubling times, the only person who can provide guidance is... *you*!

		Hegemony is an asymmetrical board game for 2 to 4 players, with games that last between 2 and 3 hours on BGA.

	Step #2
	
		In Hegemony, you play as the [red]*Working Class (WC)*[/red], the *Middle Class (MC)*, the [blue]*Capitalist Class (CC)*[/blue], or *the State*.

		Each player has a different set of abilities and their own agenda to fight for.

		In this tutorial, we will play a two-player game as the [red]Working Class[/red], and we will fight some [blue]Capitalists[/blue]!
		
	Step #3
	
		The *Middle Class* appears in 3- and 4-player games.
		*The State* is controlled by a player only in 4-player games, but it is present in every game.

		But before going into specifics about the classes, let's explain how the game is played overall.
		
	Step #4
	
		Hegemony is played in 5 *Rounds*.

		Each round is divided into several *phases*:
		- The Preparation Phase 
		- The Action Phase
		- The Production Phase
		- The Elections phase
		- The Scoring Phase 
		
	Step #5
	
		During the *Preparation Phase*, we draw cards, move the Round marker, and resolve everything each class and the State must do at the beginning of a round. The first round is handled during Game Setup.
		
	Step #6
	
		The *Action Phase* is where most of the fun happens.

		We will play cards to interact with the board and other players.

		At the start of this phase, each player has 7 cards.
		
	Step #7
	
		Players take *turns* in this order: the [red]Working Class[/red], the Middle Class, the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue], and then the State.
		
	Step #8
	
		During its turn, a player must play a card either for its effect or to perform a *Basic Action*.

		A player may also take one *Free Action*, either before or after their *Main Action*.

		Once all players have finished their turns, the sequence repeats until everyone has taken five turns. Then we *[red]STOP![/red]*

		*We take only 5 turns out of the 7 cards*. The two remaining cards are kept for the next round.
		
	Step #9
	
		Next comes the *Production Phase*: workers work at their workplaces in exchange for wages, producing goods and services for the company owners.

		Then the population must be fed, and the State must exto— I mean, collect taxes to avoid bankruptcy.
		
	Step #10
	
		As in the Preparation and Scoring Phases, almost everything is handled by BGA, but players may still have choices to make.

		(For example: if the Population needs more food than you currently have, whom shall you buy it from?)
		
	Step #11
	
		After production, it's time for elections!
		It's the *Elections Phase*.

		For each policy with a proposed bill, an election is held in order, from Fiscal Policy through Immigration.

		…but we'll see how elections work later.
		There will be cubes. Lots of them.
		
	Step #12
	
		The round concludes with a nice little phase where nothing bad happens: the *Scoring Phase*.

		During this phase, each player scores points according to their board state.

		At the end of Round 5, we also add the end-game points, and the game is over. One class will emerge victorious… may it be yours!

	Step #36
	
		*1- The fiscal policy*
		It determines how many companies the State owns. 
		It also determines how many loans the State can take before the IMF declares it bankrupt (something the Capitalists love to see).

		When this policy changes, the State must buy or sell companies using its funds.
		
	Step #37
	
		*2- Labor Market*
		It sets the minimum wage that companies must pay their workers.

		When it decreases, the Companies can change their Wage levels (provided they do not have committed Workers).
		
	Step #38

		*3- Taxation*
		How much shall we make the classes pay?

		This policy affects how the classes are taxed and defines the base value for the Tax Multiplier and its Welfare Factor.

		The Employment *Tax Multiplier* is the tax each *operational company* must pay to the State.

		The Welfare Factor multiplies the *Tax Multiplier increments* from the Welfare policies below (4 and 5).

		I will show the different taxes during the Production Phase.

	Step #39
	
		Next are *Policies 4 and 5: the Welfare State policies.*

		They determine how much the services offered by the State actually cost.

		- [red]*Socialism* (A)[/red]: they cost nothing to the buyer, but the tax multiplier is incremented by 2 times the Welfare factor given in the Taxation policy (3)

		- *Balanced* (B): they cost 5 Vardis, and the tax multiplier is incremented by the Welfare factor given in the Taxation policy (3)

		- [blue]*Neo-liberalism* (C)[/blue]: they cost 10 Vardis, and the tax multiplier is unaffected.

		When these policies change, the [blue]*Capitalist Class*[/blue] and the *Middle Class* can adjust the prices of their goods and services.

		[blue][tip] For example, if 3A, 4A and 5A are applied, then the tax multiplier would be : 3 + 2x2 + 2x2 = 11 : the maximum value.

		[tip] But if we have 3C, 4A and 5A, then the tax multiplier would be only 1 + 2x0 + 2x0 = 1 : the minimum value. (Those policies will likely lead to State bankruptcy)[/blue]
		
	Step #40
	
		Policies 6 and 7 are double-edged for everyone.
		
	Step #41
	
		*6 - Foreign trade:*

		This policy sets the tariffs on imports from and exports to the Foreign Market.

		- *High tariffs* (A): Buying from abroad becomes more expensive, encouraging the [red]Working Class[/red] to buy from the State or the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue]. It also provides the State with funds.

		- *No tariffs* (C): Buying from abroad becomes cheaper, so the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue] and [red]Working Class[/red] can buy what they need there. The downside is that the State receives less money.

		When this policy changes, the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue] and the Middle Class can adjust their goods and services prices.

		The Strike Basic Action allows the Working Class to target up to two companies.
		
	Step #92	
		
		Among the Basic Actions, you can also *Assign Workers*.

		We can assign uncommitted workers, including unemployed workers, but we can assign only up to three of them.
		(if a company isn't operating, all remaining workers become unemployed)

		When assigning workers, you may assign them to *Trade Unions* [red]if four other workers are assigned to the same sector[/red]. 

		Trade Union slots are specialized, and workers placed there are always committed.

		They have several uses..
		
	Step #93
	
		Benefits of operating Trade Unions:

		*At Production Phase*, the Working Class gains 1 Influence per "operating" Trade Union

		*At Scoring Phase*, the Working Class gains 2 VP per "operating" Trade Union

		*During demonstrations*, if at Production Phase the demonstration is still in effect, "operating" Trade Unions increase the amount of VP your opponents will lose.
		
		Some *Cards* also grant the Working Class more benefits if they have Trade Unions.
		
	Step #94
	
		Finally, there is *Applying Political Pressure*, which means adding 3 cubes from the game supply to the voting bag. 

		It's an action all classes can take (like Propose Bill).

	Step #95

		A loan gives you 50 Vardis, but you pay 5 Vardis in interest at the start of each round. Any loan still outstanding at the end of the game costs the Capitalist Class 5 VP.

		(Other classes can repay loans during the final round's Scoring Phase for 55 Vardis each. For every 5 Vardis still owed, they lose 1 VP.)

		Repaying a loan is a *Free Action*.

		- Upgradeable companies, which are operated by workers but have a gear icon indicating a production bonus (such as the Supermarket)

		We're almost at the end. Let's quickly talk about the *Middle Class* (in 3- or 4-player games) and the *State* as a player (in 4-player games).
		
	Step #105
	
		The *Middle Class* combines features of the [red]Working Class[/red] and the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue]. It favors Balanced policies.

		Like the [red]Working Class[/red], it has workers and Prosperity, and can buy goods and services.

		Like the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue], it also owns companies.

		[img]https://snipboard.io/9ediRJ.jpg[/img]
		
	Step #106
	
		The *Middle Class* does not have Business Deals, Trade Unions, Strikes, or Demonstrations, but it can sell to the Foreign Market.

		Instead, it can use the goods and services it produces for free, and it can make its workers do an Extra Shift at one of its companies as a Basic Action.

		Moreover, it earns Victory Points for each transaction when exporting goods.

		During the Election Phase, it adds a number of cubes equal to the higher of:
		- half its Population (like the [red]Working Class[/red])
		- half the number of operational companies (like the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue])
		
	Step #107
	
		Finally, its companies can be inactive, operational, or fully operational.

		A *fully operational company* is a company with at least *two operating workers*.

		During the Preparation Phase, it loses 2 Prosperity instead of 1.

		During the Scoring Phase, if its Prosperity is lower than the number of its *fully operational companies*, it gains 1 Prosperity.

		(and the only case in which a company can have both workers and empty slots)
