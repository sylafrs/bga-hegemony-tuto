	Step #4
	
		Hegemony is played over 5 *Rounds*.

		Each round is divided into several *Phases*:
		- The Preparation phase 
		- The Action phase
		- The Production phase
		- The Elections phase
		- The Scoring phase 
		
	Step #5
	
		During the *Preparation Phase*, we draw cards, move the Round marker, and do everything each Class and the State must do at the beginning of a Round (except in the 1st Round, which is replaced by Game Setup).
		
	Step #6
	
		The *Action Phase* is where most of the fun happens!

		We will play cards to interact with the board and other players.

		At the start of this phase, all players have 7 cards.
		
	Step #7
	
		Players take *turns* in order, starting with the [red]Working Class[/red], followed by the Middle Class, the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue], and then the State.
		
	Step #8
	
		During its turn, a player must play a card either for its effect or to perform a *Basic Action*.

		A player can also take one *Free Action*, which they can perform before or after the *Card Action*.

		Once all players have finished their turns, we start again, and again, and again, and again, and *[red]STOP![/red]*

		*We only play 5 turns out of the 7 cards.* The two remaining cards are kept for the next round.

		Now might be a good moment to talk about the *Policies*.

		These are game-changing rules—literally.

	Step #37
	
		*1- The fiscal policy*
		It determines how many companies the State owns. 
		It also determines how many loans the State can take before the IMF declares it bankrupt (something the Capitalists love to see).

		When this law changes, the State has to buy or sell companies using its funds.
		
	Step #38
	
		*2- Labor Market*
		It sets the minimum salary level that companies must pay.

		When the minimum salary level decreases, companies can change their salary levels (provided they do not have committed Workers).
		
	Step #39

		*3- Taxation*
		How much should we make the classes pay?

		[blue]The Tax Multiplier[/blue] that we can see on the top of the board shows the taxes *operational companies* must pay to the state.

		This policy also affects the basic taxes paid by all classes (a separate tax table is used for each class).

		Finally, it multiplies the tax multiplier increases from Policies 4 and 5 below.

	Step #40
	
		Next come Policies 4 and 5: the *Welfare State policies*.

		They determine how much the services offered by the State actually cost.

		- [red]*Socialism* (A)[/red]: services cost nothing to the buyer, but twice the Welfare Tax Multiplier is added to the Tax Multiplier.

		- *Balanced* (B): services cost 5 Vardis, and the Welfare Tax Multiplier is added to the Tax Multiplier.

		- [blue]*Neoliberalism* (C)[/blue]: services cost 10 Vardis, and the Tax Multiplier is unaffected.

		When these policies change, the [blue]*Capitalist Class*[/blue] and the *Middle Class* can change the prices of their goods and services.

		[blue][tip] For example, if 3A, 4A, and 5A are in effect, the Tax Multiplier would be 3 + 2×2 + 2×2 = 11, the maximum value.

		[tip] But if we have 3C, 4A, and 5A, the Tax Multiplier would be only 1 + 2×0 + 2×0 = 1, the minimum value. (Those policies will likely lead to State bankruptcy.)[/blue]
		
	Step #41
	
		Policies 6 and 7 are double-edged laws for everyone.
		
	Step #42
	
		*6 - Foreign trade:*

		This policy sets the tariffs on purchases from and sales to the foreign market.

		- *High tariffs* (A): Buying goods from abroad becomes expensive, encouraging the [red]Working Class[/red] to buy from the State or the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue]. It also gives the State some revenue.

		- *No tariffs* (C): Buying goods from abroad becomes cheaper, so the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue] and [red]Working Class[/red] can buy what they need there. The downside is that the State receives less revenue.

		When this policy changes, the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue] and the Middle Class can change their goods and services prices.
		
	Step #43

		Usually, the *Middle Class* and the [red]*Working Class*[/red] each get two Workers during the Preparation Phase.

		This policy adds more Workers.

		[blue][tip] For example, if 7C is in effect, the Working Class gains 4 Workers at the start of the next Round![/blue]

	Step #93
	
		Benefits of operating Trade Unions:

		*During the Production Phase*, the Working Class gains 1 Influence per "operational" Trade Union.

		*During the Scoring Phase*, the Working Class gains 2 VP per "operational" Trade Union.

		*During Demonstrations*, if the Demonstration is still in effect during the Production Phase, "operational" Trade Unions increase the number of VP your opponents lose.

	Step #104
	
		We're almost at the end: let's quickly talk about the *Middle Class* (in 3- or 4-player games) and *the State* as a player (in 4-player games).
		
	Step #105
	
		The *Middle Class* is a mix of the [red]Working Class[/red] and the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue]. It wants the laws to be Balanced.

		Like the [red]Working Class[/red], it has Workers and Prosperity, and can buy goods and services.

		And like the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue], it owns some companies.

		[img]https://snipboard.io/9ediRJ.jpg[/img]
		
	Step #106
	
		The *Middle Class* does not have Business Deals, Trade Unions, Strikes, or Demonstrations, but it can sell to the Foreign Market.

		Instead, it can buy the goods and services it produces for free, and it can make its Workers do an Extra Shift at one of its companies as a Base Action.

		Moreover, it earns Victory Points for each transaction when exporting goods.

		During the Election Phase, it adds a number of cubes equal to the higher of:
		- Half its Population (like the [red]Working Class[/red])
		- Half the number of operational companies (like the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue])
		
	Step #107
	
		Finally, its companies can be inactive, operational, or fully operational.

		A *fully operational company* is a company with at least *2 operational Workers*.

		During the Preparation Phase, it loses 2 Prosperity instead of 1.

		But during the Scoring Phase, if its Prosperity value is lower than the number of its *fully operational companies*, it gains +1 Prosperity.
		
	Step #108
	
		Some companies can operate with only 1 Middle Class Worker, and can be "upgraded" when a [red]Working Class[/red] Worker is also assigned there.

		[img]https://snipboard.io/QbUAxC.jpg[/img]

		This is the only case in which Middle Class Workers and Working Class Workers can work in the same company. 
		(and the only case in which a company can have both Workers and empty slots)

		Otherwise, the companies *must* be either empty or filled with the same type of Worker.
		
	Step #109
	
		The State is controlled by a player only in 4-player games.

		When a player controls the State, their objective is to balance the game and try to keep everyone happy. The Events drawn each Round and the State's cards help them do that.

	Step #110
	
		The State does not have voting cubes, but it can still propose Bills or use its Influence during a vote.
		It can earn the +1 VP during a vote only if it used Influence.

		The State can try to earn even more points by aligning policies with the agenda it draws each Round (1 VP per aligned policy).
