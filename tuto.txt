		Welcome to the tutorial for Hegemony: Lead your class to Victory!

		The nation is in disarray. The economy is failing and political tensions are rising. The [red]*Working Class*[/red] faces a dismantled welfare system, the [blue]*Capitalists*[/blue] are losing their profits, the middle class is gradually fading, and the state is sinking into a deep deficit. In these troubling times, the only person who can provide guidance is... *you*!

		Hegemony is an asymmetrical gameplay board game for 2 to 4 players.


	Step #2
	
		In Hegemony, you will play the [red]*Working Class (WC)*[/red], the *Middle Class (MC)*, the [blue]*Capitalist Class (CC)*[/blue] or you will play the *State*.

		Each player has a different set of abilities and their own agenda to fight for.

		In this tutorial, we will play a 2-player game as the [red]Working Class[/red], and we will fight some [blue]Capitalists[/blue]!
		
	Step #3
	
		The *Middle Class* appears in 3- and 4-player games.
		The *State* is only controlled by a player in 4-player games, but is present in all games.

		But before going into specifics about the classes, let's explain how the overall gameplay.
		
	Step #4
	
		Hegemony is played in 5 *Rounds*.

		Each round is divided in multiple *Phases*:
		- The Preparation phase 
		- The Action phase
		- The Production phase
		- The Elections phase
		- The Scoring phase 
		
	Step #5
	
		In the *Preparation phase* we draw cards, move the Round marker, and do everything a Class and the State is required to do at the beginning of a Round (except for the 1st Round which is replaced by Game Setup).
		
	Step #6
	
		The *Action phase* is where most of the fun lies.

		We will play cards to interact with the board and other players.

		At the start of this phase, all players has 7 cards.
		
	Step #7
	
		Players take *turns* in order, starting with the [red]Working Class[/red], followed by the Middle Class, the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue], and then the State.
		
	Step #8
	
		During their turn, a player must play a card either *for its effect* or to perform a *Basic Action*: this is called the *Main Action*.

		A player can also take one *Free Action* that they can perform before or after the Main Action.

		Once all players have finished their turn, we start again, and again, and again, and again, and *[red]STOP![/red]*

		*We only play 5 turns out of the 7 cards.* The two remaining cards are kept for next round.
		
	Step #9
	
		Next is the *Production Phase*: Workers work at Companies, in exchange for money, and this produces Goods & Services into the Companies' owner Storages.

		After this, the Population needs to be fed, and the State needs to exto-- I mean, collect taxes to avoid bankruptcy.
		
	Step #10
	
		Like with the Preparation and Scoring phases, almost everything is handled by BGA but players may have choices to make.

		(For example: if the Population needs more food than you currently have, whom shall you buy it from?)
		
	Step #11
	
		After production comes the time for elections!
		It's the *Elections Phase*.

		For each Policy that has a proposed bill, an election is carried out in order, starting from the Fiscal Policy to the Immigration Policy.

		..but we will see how elections work later.
		There will be cubes. Lots of them.
		
	Step #12
	
		The Round concludes with a nice little phase where nothing bad happens: the *Scoring Phase*.

		During this phase, each player earns points according to their board state.

		At the end of Round 5, we will also add the end of game points and the game will be over, a class will rise to victory... May it be yours.

	Step #35
	
		Now might be a good moment to talk about the *Policies*.

		These are game-changing rules —literally.

		There are 7 of them, including 5 "main" policies that go from [red]socialism (left, "A")[/red] to [blue]neoliberalism (right, "C")[/blue], with the middle column ("B") representing a compromise between the two.

*The Middle Class* loves compromises.

	Step #36
	
		*1- Fiscal Policy*
		It determines how many companies the State owns. 
		It also determines how many loans the State can take before the IMF declares it bankrupt (something the Capitalists love to see).

		When this policy changes, the State MUST buy or sell companies using its funds.
		
	Step #37
	
		*2- Labor Market*
		It sets the minimal Wage level the Companies must pay their Workers.

		When it decreases, the Companies can change their Wage levels (provided they do not have committed Workers).
		
	Step #38

		*3- Taxation*
		How much shall we make the classes pay?

		This policy affects the way the classes are taxed, and defines the base value for the Tax Multiplier and its Welfare factor.

		The Employment *Tax Multiplier* is the tax each *operational Company* must pay to the State.

		The Welfare factor multiplies the *Tax multiplier increments* from the Welfare policies below (4 and 5).

		I will show the different taxes during the Production Phase.

	Step #39
	
		Next are *4 and 5: The Welfare State policies.*

		They determine how much the services offered by the State actually cost.

		- [red]*Socialism* (A)[/red]: they cost nothing to the buyer, but the tax multiplier is incremented by 2 times the Welfare factor given in the Taxation policy (3)

		- *Balanced* (B): they cost 5 Vardis, and the tax multiplier is incremented by the Welfare factor given in the Taxation policy (3)

		- [blue]*Neo-liberalism* (C)[/blue]: they cost 10 Vardis, and the tax multiplier is unaffected.

		When those change, the [blue]*Capitalist*[/blue] and *Middle* classes can change the prices of their Goods & Services.

		[blue][tip] For example, if 3A, 4A and 5A are applied, then the tax multiplier would be : 3 + 2x2 + 2x2 = 11 : the maximum value.

		[tip] But if we have 3C, 4A and 5A, then the tax multiplier would be only 1 + 2x0 + 2x0 = 1 : the minimum value. (Those policies will likely lead to State bankruptcy)[/blue]
		
	Step #40
	
		Policies 6 and 7 are double-edged laws for everyone.
		
	Step #41
	
		*6 - Foreign trade:*

		This policy sets the tariffs on deals and imports from the Foreign Market.

		- *High tariffs* (A): Buying from abroad becomes more expensive, encouraging the [red]Working Class[/red] to buy from the State or the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue]. It also provides the State with funds.

		- *No tariffs* (C): Buying from abroad becomes cheaper, so the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue] and [red]Working Class[/red] can buy what they need there. The downside is that the State receives less money.

		When this policy changes, the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue] and the Middle Class can adjust their goods and services prices.
		
	Step #42
	
		*7 - Immigration*

		Usually, the *Middle class* and the [red]*Working class*[/red] each gain two Workers during Preparation Phase.

		This policy increase this amount of Workers.

		[blue][tip] For example, if 7C is in effect, the Working class gains 4 Workers at the start of the next Round, instead of two![/blue]

	Step #91

	Step #93
	
		Operating Trade Union benefits:

		*At Scoring Phase*, the Working class gains 2 VP per "operating" Trade Union

	Step #95

	Step #104
	
		This is almost the end: let's quickly talk about the *Middle Class* (3/4 players) and The *State* as a player (4 players).
		
	Step #105
	
		The *Middle Class* is a mix of the [red]Working Class[/red] and the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue]. They want the laws to be Balanced.

		Like the [red]Working class[/red] they have workers, a prosperity, can buy goods and services.

		And like the [blue]Capitalist class[/blue] they have some companies on their own.

		[img]https://snipboard.io/9ediRJ.jpg[/img]
		
	Step #106
	
		The *Middle Class* don't have Business Deals, Trade Unions, Strikes or Demonstrations at their disposal but they can sell to the Foreign Market.

		Instead they can buy the Good and Services they produce for free, and they can force their workers to do an Extra Shift on one of their company as a Base Action.

		Moreover, they earn victory points for each transaction when they export goods.

		During Election Phase, they add cubes equal to the higher value between :
		- Half the population (like the [red]Working Class[/red])
		- Half the number of operational Companies (like the [blue]Capitalist Class[/blue])
		
	Step #107
	
		Finally their companies can be inactive, operated, or fully operated.

		A *fully operated company* is a company with at least *2 operating workers*.

		At Preparation Phase, they lose 2 Prosperity instead of 1.

		But at Scoring Phase, if their Prosperity value is lower than the count of their *fully operated companies*, they earn +1 Prosperity.
		
	Step #108
	
		Some companies can be operated with only 1 Middle Class worker, and can be "upgraded" when a [red]Working Class[/red] worker is also affected.

		[img]https://snipboard.io/QbUAxC.jpg[/img]

		This is the only case in which Middle class workers and Working class workers are working in the same company. 
		(and the only case in which we can have workers and empty slots)

		Otherwise, the companies *must* be empty or filled with the same type of worker.
		
	Step #109
	
		The *State* is only controlled by a player on 4-player games.

		When a player is controlling them, their objective is to balance the game, trying to make everybody happy. The events drawn each Round and their cards help them do that.

		[img]https://x.boardgamearena.net/data/tutorials/img6ad5b1e7f3f112.43266956.jpg[/img]
		
	Step #110
	
		The State don't have voting cubes but can still propose bills or use their Influence during a vote.
		This means they can only win the +1 VP during a vote only if they used Influence.

		The State can try to gain even more points by aligning the policies to the agenda card they draw each Round. (1 VP per aligned Policy.)

		[img]https://snipboard.io/zU5X7d.jpg[/img]
